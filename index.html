Scope and accuracy: LabelFit reports candidate transcriptions with model-provided snippets. It does not issue food-safety verdicts, diagnose, interpret allergy risk, or tell caregivers what to feed. Small, curved, or blurry print can be misread, so compare every readout with the package.

WHY THIS FOCUS

One label-reading workflow, built around baby food.

Open Food Facts offers broad product records and label-derived information; general OCR tools read package text. LabelFit focuses on packaged complementary foods for babies 6+ months, keeping each extracted detail tied to a photo snippet and surfacing uncertain reads. The current prototype transcribes label details; product lookups and plain-language explanations are later steps.

BUILD ROADMAP

So far: an exploratory 10-package check on mixed grocery products (only one is a complementary food) against founder-entered text. It is not an accuracy claim.

Next: a source-backed benchmark on packaged complementary foods for 6+ months, measuring field accuracy, wrong “Readable” reads, and review time.

Later: after accuracy, usability, and privacy reviews, test whether retailers or food brands value label-digitization tools. No revenue or partner traction is established.
